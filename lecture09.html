Lecture 8 — Kinetics of Rigid Bodies

You applied Newton's laws to rigid bodies: ΣF = m·a_G for translation and ΣM_G = I_G·α for rotation. You handled rolling without slip using the kinematic constraint a_G = R·α, and applied the parallel axis theorem to find I_O for pinned bodies.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Express total kinetic energy of a rigid body as translation plus rotation T = ½m·v_G² + ½I_G·ω². Both terms are always present for general plane motion. For pure rotation about a fixed point use T = ½I_O·ω². Rolling friction does zero work — the contact point has zero velocity.
  2. Calculate work done by moments and couples Work done by a constant couple M rotating through angle θ: U = M·θ. This is the rotational analogue of W = F·d. For rolling bodies, friction at the contact point does zero work.
  3. Apply angular momentum conservation to rigid body collisions Angular momentum H_G is conserved when ΣM_G = 0. For eccentric impact (off-center collision), combine: linear momentum conservation + angular momentum conservation + the coefficient of restitution equation.
Caution ⚠

Friction in rolling without slip does zero work — do not subtract a friction work term from the energy equation. Angular momentum conservation requires zero net external moment, not zero net external force. Eccentric impact needs three equations, not two — missing the angular equation is the most common error.
