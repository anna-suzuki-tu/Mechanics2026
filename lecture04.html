Lecture 3 — Newton's Laws & FBD

You applied Newton's second law using FBDs and strategic axis choices. You can now set up equations of motion for particles on slopes, in circular motion, and in constraint systems. Today we introduce scalar methods that bypass force analysis entirely.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Select the right scalar method before calculating Displacement/height given → work-energy method. Time or collision given → impulse-momentum method. Never start calculating before choosing the method — the wrong choice costs you the entire solution.
  2. Apply the work-energy theorem including non-conservative forces T₁ + ΣU₁₋₂ = T₂. When friction is present, subtract its work: T₁ + V₁ − W_friction = T₂ + V₂. Mechanical energy is conserved only when all forces are conservative.
  3. Solve collision problems using momentum conservation and COR Apply linear momentum conservation first. Then add the COR equation: e = (relative separation speed)/(relative approach speed). Two equations give two unknown post-collision velocities.
Caution ⚠

Mechanical energy conservation FAILS when friction is present — always check for non-conservative forces first. The COR equation gives the relative velocity relationship; it does not replace momentum conservation. Power is P = F·v, not F·d.

🟢 Basic Problems — Explained in Main Video
Basic 1
Work Done → Final Speed
A 5 kg block starts from rest on a frictionless horizontal surface. A constant force F = 15 N is applied over a displacement d = 3 m. Use the work-energy theorem to find the final speed.
Basic 2
Change in Kinetic Energy
A 2 kg particle moves at v₁ = 3 m/s. A net force does W = 25 J of work on it. Find the final speed v₂. Hint: ΔKE = W.
🟡 Standard Problems — Core
Standard 1
Incline with Friction — Two Methods
A 10 kg block slides down a 5 m ramp at 30° with μ_k = 0.25. Find the speed at the bottom using: (1) the work-energy method and (2) Newton's second law. Verify both answers match. Hint: friction does negative work; W_friction = −μ_k N·d.
Standard 2
Elastic Collision
Ball A (2 kg, v = 6 m/s) strikes stationary ball B (2 kg) head-on. The collision is perfectly elastic (e = 1). Find v_A and v_B after the collision. Hint: use momentum conservation AND the COR equation simultaneously.
Standard 3
Impulse and Average Force
A 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and bounces back at 8 m/s. Contact time is Δt = 0.02 s. Find: (1) the impulse from the wall, and (2) the average force from the wall on the ball.
🔴 Advanced Problem — Optional
Advanced 1
Three-Phase Energy Chain
A 3 kg block starts from rest at the top of a frictionless 30° incline (length 4 m). It hits a spring (k = 500 N/m) at the bottom, compresses it, then rebounds. Find: (1) the speed at the bottom just before the spring, (2) the maximum spring compression, (3) the speed when the block returns to the top of the incline. Apply energy conservation at each phase transition.
