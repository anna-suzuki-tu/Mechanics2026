Lecture 6 — Kinematics of Rigid Bodies

You covered rigid body kinematics: the relative velocity equation v_B = v_A + ω × r_B/A, the ICR method for fast velocity analysis, and the full acceleration equation including the centripetal term. You can now find the velocity and acceleration of any point on a rigid body in plane motion.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Write the two equations of motion for a rigid body in plane motion ΣF = m·a_G (translation of mass center) and ΣM_G = I_G·α (rotation about mass center). These two vector equations are always applied together — never one without the other.
  2. Apply the parallel axis theorem to find moment of inertia about any point I_O = I_G + m·d², where d is the distance from G to point O. Use this when taking moments about a fixed pin or the contact point of a rolling body. I_G values for standard shapes must be known.
  3. Handle rolling without slipping as a kinematic constraint For a wheel rolling without slip: a_G = R·α and v_G = R·ω. This constraint links translation and rotation — reducing the unknowns by one. The contact point has zero velocity but nonzero acceleration.
Caution ⚠

The moment equation ΣM = I·α is only valid about the mass center G, or about a fixed point O using I_O. Taking moments about a random moving point without correction gives wrong results. Always identify whether the body is rolling without slip (constraint applies) or slipping (use kinetic friction instead).
