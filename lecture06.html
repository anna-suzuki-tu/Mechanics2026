Lecture 5 — System of Particles & Center of Mass

You extended Newton's laws to systems of particles: only external forces drive the mass center, and linear momentum is conserved when ΣF_ext = 0. Internal forces always cancel. Now we extend further — from point masses to rigid bodies that have shape and can rotate.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Decompose general plane motion into translation plus rotation Any rigid body motion = translation of a reference point A + rotation about A with angular velocity ω. The relative velocity formula v_B = v_A + ω × r_B/A always holds. v_B/A is always perpendicular to line AB.
  2. Use the Instantaneous Center of Rotation (ICR) for fast velocity analysis The ICR is the point with zero velocity at a given instant. All other points have speed proportional to their distance from the ICR. The ICR method gives velocities in one step — but it cannot be used for acceleration.
  3. Apply the full vector equation for acceleration analysis a_B = a_A + α × r_B/A − ω²r_B/A. The last term is the centripetal component, always directed toward A. The ICR method cannot be extended to acceleration — always use the full equation.
Caution ⚠

The ICR changes position over time — it is instantaneous. Never use the same ICR for two different time steps. The contact point of a rolling wheel has zero velocity but nonzero acceleration (directed upward toward the center). v = 0 does NOT mean a = 0.

🟢 Basic Problems — Explained in Main Video
Basic 1
Velocity of a Point on a Moving Link
Rigid link AB (length 0.5 m, horizontal) has pin A moving right at v_A = 4 m/s. The link rotates at ω = 2 rad/s clockwise. Find the velocity of B using v_B = v_A + ω × r_B/A. Note: ω × r_B/A is perpendicular to AB.
Basic 2
Rolling Wheel — Velocity at Key Points
A wheel of radius r = 0.4 m rolls without slipping. Its center C moves at v_C = 2 m/s to the right. Find the velocity of: (a) the contact point P (bottom), and (b) the top point T. Hint: use v = v_C + ω × r for each point; no-slip gives ω = v_C/r.
🟡 Standard Problems — Core
Standard 1
Sliding Bar — ICR Method
A 1 m bar AB slides with end A on the floor and end B against a vertical wall. At the instant shown, A moves to the right at 3 m/s and the bar makes a 30° angle with the horizontal. Use the ICR method to find ω and v_B. Hint: the ICR lies at the intersection of lines drawn perpendicular to each end's velocity.
Standard 2
Crank-Slider Mechanism
Crank OA (length 0.3 m) rotates at ω_OA = 10 rad/s counterclockwise. At the instant when OA is vertical (pointing up), connecting rod AB (length 0.5 m) is horizontal. Find the velocity of slider B using the relative velocity method: v_B = v_A + ω_AB × r_B/A.
Standard 3
Two-Link Mechanism
Links AB and BC (each 0.6 m). A is pinned to the ground; C slides horizontally. At the instant when AB is vertical and BC is horizontal, link AB rotates at ω_AB = 3 rad/s counterclockwise. Find ω_BC and v_C. Hint: v_C = v_B + ω_BC × r_C/B; v_C must be horizontal.
🔴 Advanced Problem — Optional
Advanced 1
Acceleration Analysis — Why ICR Fails
Using the same sliding bar from Standard 1 (A moves at constant 3 m/s, so a_A = 0), find the acceleration of point B. Use a_B = a_A + α × r_B/A − ω²r_B/A. Then explain why the ICR method cannot give acceleration: the ICR is changing position, so it has its own acceleration — the ICR is not a fixed center.
