Lectures 6–8 — Rigid Body Mechanics (+ Midterm)

Lectures 6–8 completed rigid body mechanics: kinematics, Newton's equations, and energy/momentum methods for rotating systems. The midterm covered Chapters 11–17. This lecture begins the third and final part of the course: vibration and analytical mechanics.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Derive the equation of motion and find the natural frequency Apply Newton's second law to get mẍ + kx = 0. The natural frequency is ω_n = √(k/m) rad/s. This depends only on system properties — not on initial conditions or how hard the system is excited.
  2. Write and interpret the full response x(t) from initial conditions General solution: x(t) = C₁cos(ω_n t) + C₂sin(ω_n t). Apply x(0) = x₀ → C₁ = x₀; ẋ(0) = v₀ → C₂ = v₀/ω_n. Amplitude A = √(C₁² + C₂²). Period T = 2π/ω_n.
  3. Understand energy exchange in SHM and the concept of resonance At equilibrium: KE is maximum, PE = 0. At the extremes: KE = 0, PE is maximum. Total energy E = ½kA² = constant. Resonance occurs when the forcing frequency matches ω_n — amplitude grows without bound in the undamped case.
Caution ⚠

At the extreme positions (x = ±A), velocity is zero but acceleration is maximum: a = −ω_n²A. Never confuse v = 0 with a = 0. The natural frequency ω_n does not change with amplitude — it is a property of the system, not the motion.
