Lectures 6–8 — Rigid Body Mechanics (+ Midterm)

Lectures 6–8 completed rigid body mechanics: kinematics, Newton's equations, and energy/momentum methods for rotating systems. The midterm covered Chapters 11–17. This lecture begins the third and final part of the course: vibration and analytical mechanics.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Derive the equation of motion for an undamped spring-mass system and find its natural frequency Apply Newton's second law to get mẍ + kx = 0. The natural frequency is ω_n = √(k/m) rad/s. This depends only on system properties — not on initial conditions or how hard the system is excited.
  2. Write and interpret the full response x(t) from initial conditions General solution: x(t) = C₁cos(ω_n t) + C₂sin(ω_n t). Apply x(0) = x₀ → C₁ = x₀; ẋ(0) = v₀ → C₂ = v₀/ω_n. Amplitude A = √(C₁² + C₂²). Period T = 2π/ω_n.
  3. Understand energy exchange in SHM and the concept of resonance At equilibrium: KE is maximum, PE = 0. At the extremes: KE = 0, PE is maximum. Total energy E = ½kA² = constant. Resonance occurs when the forcing frequency matches ω_n — amplitude grows without bound in the undamped case.
Caution ⚠

At the extreme positions (x = ±A), velocity is zero but acceleration is maximum: a = −ω_n²A. Never confuse v = 0 with a = 0. The natural frequency ω_n does not change with amplitude — it is a property of the system, not the motion.

Basic 1
Natural Frequency
A spring-mass system has m = 2 kg and k = 200 N/m. Find: (1) the natural angular frequency ω_n (rad/s), (2) the natural frequency f_n (Hz), and (3) the period T_n (s).
Basic 2
Simple Pendulum
Derive ω_n for a simple pendulum of length L = 0.5 m using the small-angle approximation. Show that the result equals √(g/L) and explain why the pendulum shares the same equation structure as a spring-mass system.
🟡 Standard Problems — Core
Standard 1
Initial Value Problem
For m = 1 kg, k = 100 N/m, with initial conditions x(0) = 0.05 m and ẋ(0) = 0.3 m/s: (1) find ω_n, (2) find C₁ and C₂ in x(t) = C₁cos(ω_n t) + C₂sin(ω_n t), (3) compute the amplitude A = √(C₁² + C₂²).
Standard 2
Energy Conservation Check
Using the result from Standard 1 (A ≈ 0.0583 m, ω_n = 10 rad/s): (1) compute V_max = ½kA², (2) compute v_max = A·ω_n and KE_max = ½mv_max², (3) verify numerically that V_max = KE_max.
Standard 3
Vertical Spring
A mass m = 5 kg hangs from a vertical spring (k = 500 N/m). (1) Find the static deflection δ_st. (2) Write the equation of motion in y (displacement from equilibrium) and find ω_n. (3) Show that ω_n = √(g/δ_st).

Advanced 1
Pinned Rod Oscillation
