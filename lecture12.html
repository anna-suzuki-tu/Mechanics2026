Lecture 11 — Analytical Mechanics I: Why Lagrangian?

You identified Newton's limitations for constrained systems. You defined generalized coordinates and the Lagrangian L = T − V. You experienced how constraint forces disappear automatically when using scalar energy quantities, deriving the spring-mass equation of motion in three lines without a force diagram.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Derive and apply the Euler-Lagrange equation The Euler-Lagrange equation: d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) − ∂L/∂q = 0. The procedure is always the same: compute ∂L/∂q̇, differentiate with respect to time, then subtract ∂L/∂q. Apply it to each generalized coordinate independently.
  2. Recognize cyclic coordinates and their conservation law A coordinate q is cyclic if L does not depend on q — only on q̇. In that case, ∂L/∂q̇ = constant, meaning the corresponding generalized momentum is conserved. This is a direct route to conservation laws.
  3. Apply the Lagrangian method to multi-DOF systems and rigid bodies For a system with n degrees of freedom, write n Lagrange equations — one per generalized coordinate. Constraint forces that do no work never appear. This is why the Lagrangian scales to complex systems where Newton's method becomes intractable.
Caution ⚠

The Euler-Lagrange equation requires differentiating ∂L/∂q̇ with respect to TIME — not with respect to q. This is d/dt(∂L/∂q̇), not ∂²L/∂q̇². For non-conservative forces (friction, external loads), add the generalized force Q_i to the right side: d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) − ∂L/∂q = Q_i.

🟢 Basic Problems — Explained in Main Video
Basic 1
Simple Pendulum
A simple pendulum has bob mass m and rod length l. The generalized coordinate is θ (angle from vertical). The Lagrangian is L = ½ml²θ̇² + mgl cosθ. Apply the Euler-Lagrange equation and derive the equation of motion. Note: rod tension never appears in this method.
Basic 2
Coupled Oscillators
Two masses m₁ and m₂ are connected in series by springs k₁ (wall to m₁) and k₂ (m₁ to m₂). Generalized coordinates: x₁ and x₂ (displacements from equilibrium). T = ½m₁ẋ₁² + ½m₂ẋ₂², V = ½k₁x₁² + ½k₂(x₂ − x₁)². Apply the Euler-Lagrange equation to each coordinate and derive the two equations of motion. Note: the cross term in V automatically generates the coupling terms with correct signs.
Basic 3
Rolling Cylinder on an Inclined Plane
A uniform cylinder (mass m, radius r, I_G = ½mr²) rolls without slipping down a slope of angle φ. Use s (displacement of the center along the slope) as the generalized coordinate. The rolling constraint ṡ = rθ̇ is absorbed into s, so θ̇ = ṡ/r. Write L = ¾mṡ² + mgs sinφ, apply the Euler-Lagrange equation, and find the acceleration a_G. Note: friction force never appears — it does no virtual work once the rolling constraint is absorbed into s.
🟡 Core Problems
Core 1
Euler-Lagrange Procedure: Spring-Mass System
For a spring-mass system, the Lagrangian is L = ½mẋ² − ½kx². Apply the Euler-Lagrange equation step by step: compute ∂L/∂ẋ, differentiate with respect to time, subtract ∂L/∂x. Derive the equation of motion and verify that the result matches Newton's second law F = ma.
Core 2
Cart with Pendulum
A cart of mass M is connected to a fixed wall by a spring of stiffness k and can slide horizontally. A pendulum (mass m, length l) hangs from the cart. Use x (cart displacement) and θ (pendulum angle) as generalized coordinates. For small θ, the Lagrangian is approximately L ≈ ½(M+m)ẋ² + mlẋθ̇ + ½ml²θ̇² − ½kx² + mgl(1 − θ²/2). (1) Derive the equation of motion for x. (2) Derive the equation of motion for θ. Note: the coupling term mlẋθ̇ in T automatically produces cross-coupling in both equations.
Core 3
Cyclic Coordinate and Conservation Law
A particle of mass m moves under a central force V = V(r) in a plane. In polar coordinates (r, θ), the Lagrangian is L = ½m(ṙ² + r²θ̇²) − V(r). (1) Identify which coordinate is cyclic and explain why. (2) State the conservation law that follows, and identify the conserved quantity physically. (3) Write the Euler-Lagrange equation for r (the non-cyclic coordinate). Hint: a coordinate q is cyclic if ∂L/∂q = 0.
🔴 Advanced Problem — Optional
Advanced 1
Double Pendulum
A double pendulum has rod lengths L₁, L₂ and masses m₁ (at end of rod 1) and m₂ (at end of rod 2), with angles θ₁ and θ₂ measured from the vertical. (1) Write T and V exactly in terms of θ₁, θ₂, θ̇₁, θ̇₂. (2) For small amplitudes (sinθ ≈ θ, cosθ ≈ 1), derive the two linearized equations of motion via the Euler-Lagrange equation. (3) Describe what Newton's method would require to obtain the same result — how many free-body diagrams, force equations, and unknowns are needed?
