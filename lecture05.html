Lecture 4 — Work, Energy & Momentum

You learned to choose between scalar methods: work-energy (displacement-based) and impulse-momentum (time or collision-based). You solved collision problems using momentum conservation and the coefficient of restitution. Today we extend these ideas from a single particle to a system of particles.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Understand why internal forces cancel and only external forces drive the mass center By Newton's third law, every internal force has an equal and opposite partner — their sum is always zero. The mass center G accelerates only due to external forces: ΣF_ext = m·a_G.
  2. Identify the conditions for linear momentum conservation If ΣF_ext = 0, the total linear momentum L = m·v_G is constant. This applies during explosions, separations, and collisions. Always check the external force condition before applying conservation.
  3. Track the mass center through explosions and separations An internal explosion cannot change the trajectory of the mass center. After a fireworks shell explodes, its mass center continues on the same parabola as before. Only external forces determine where G goes.
Caution ⚠

Internal forces (explosion, spring release) do NOT change the total momentum of the system — they only redistribute it among fragments. Linear momentum conservation requires zero net external force; gravity often violates this condition. Always draw a system boundary first and identify what is inside.

Basic 1
Position of the Center of Mass
Two masses: m₁ = 2 kg at x₁ = 1 m and m₂ = 6 kg at x₂ = 5 m. Find the position of the center of mass x_G.
Basic 2
Internal Forces Cannot Move the Mass Center
Two identical 3 kg masses A and B are connected by a compressed spring on a frictionless surface. Both are initially at rest. After release, A moves left at 3 m/s. (1) Find the velocity of B. (2) What happens to the mass center? (3) Why?
🟡 Standard Problems — Core
Standard 1
Explosion — Momentum Conservation
A 10 kg shell is launched at v₀ = 20 m/s horizontally. At its highest point (v_y = 0) it explodes into two 5 kg fragments. Fragment 1 falls straight down (horizontal velocity = 0). Find the velocity of Fragment 2. Hint: apply linear momentum conservation at the instant of explosion. What is the total horizontal momentum just before?
Standard 2
Astronaut Recoil
A 60 kg astronaut floating at rest in space throws a 2 kg tool at 10 m/s (relative to the ground). Find the astronaut's recoil velocity. State the principle used and check whether the required condition is satisfied.
Standard 3
Rocket Thrust
A rocket has initial total mass M = 2000 kg (including 500 kg of fuel). It burns fuel at ṁ = 20 kg/s with exhaust velocity v_e = 3000 m/s relative to the rocket. Find the thrust force. Ignore gravity. Hint: thrust = ṁ · v_e.
🔴 Advanced Problem — Optional
Advanced 1
Kinetic Energy Decomposition
Two particles on a line: m₁ = 3 kg with v₁ = 4 m/s (right), m₂ = 5 kg with v₂ = −2 m/s (left). Calculate: (1) total KE of the system, (2) KE of the mass center, (3) KE relative to the mass center. Verify that (1) = (2) + (3). What does this decomposition tell you about internal energy?
