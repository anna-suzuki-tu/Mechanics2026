Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Identify the limitations of Newton's method that motivate analytical mechanics Newton's method requires identifying and resolving all constraint forces (normal forces, tensions) as explicit vectors. For systems with many degrees of freedom or complex constraints, this becomes unmanageable. Analytical mechanics replaces force vectors with scalar energy quantities.
  2. Define generalized coordinates and the Lagrangian L = T − V A generalized coordinate q is any independent variable that fully describes the system configuration. The Lagrangian L = T − V is the difference between kinetic and potential energy. Its form is the same regardless of which coordinates you choose.
  3. Experience the computational advantage on a simple system For a spring-mass system: write T = ½mẋ², V = ½kx², L = T − V. The equation of motion falls out in three lines — without drawing a single force diagram. Compare directly with Newton's method on the same problem.
Caution ⚠

The Lagrangian L = T − V is NOT the total energy (which is T + V). The sign difference is essential — using T + V gives the wrong equations of motion. A generalized coordinate must be independent; if constraints link your chosen variables, you have too many coordinates.
