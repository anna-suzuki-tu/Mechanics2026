Course Overview

Welcome to Mechanics 2026. This is the first lecture — no prior lecture content is required beyond high school physics. This course will take you from particle kinematics through rigid body dynamics to the foundations of analytical mechanics.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Master the calculus chain of motion Differentiate position to get velocity; differentiate velocity to get acceleration. Integrate in reverse. You can derive every kinematic formula from first principles — no memorization needed.
  2. Classify acceleration type before calculating Identify whether acceleration is constant, a function of time a(t), or a function of position a(x). Each type requires a different method. Choosing the wrong one produces a wrong answer.
  3. Apply the chain rule identity for a(x) problems Use a = v dv/dx to handle problems where time is not given (e.g., braking distance). This identity also previews the work-energy theorem in Lecture 4.
Caution ⚠

v = 0 does NOT mean a = 0. A ball at its peak has zero velocity but acceleration of −9.81 m/s². The constant-acceleration formulas are ONLY valid when a is truly constant — never apply them to a(t) or a(x) problems.

Basic 1
Differentiation Chain
A particle's position is given by x(t) = 3t² + 2t. Find: (1) the velocity v(t), and (2) the acceleration a(t).
Basic 2
Re-deriving Constant Acceleration Formulas
Given constant acceleration a = 4 and initial conditions v(0) = v₀, x(0) = x₀, derive v(t) and x(t) using integration.
🟡 Standard Problems — Core
Standard 1
Acceleration as a Function of Time
a(t) = 6t, with v(0) = 0, x(0) = 0. Find v(t) and x(t).
Standard 2
Acceleration as a Function of Position
a(x) = 2x, with x = 0, v = 0. Find v(x). Hint: use a = v dv/dx.
Standard 3
Velocity-Dependent Acceleration
a = −kv. Find v(t). Hint: separate variables.
🔴 Advanced Problem — Optional
Advanced 1
Bridge to Energy
a(x) = −kx. Find v(x). Notice what the result tells you about energy conservation.
