Lecture 1 — Kinematics of a Particle

You rebuilt the calculus chain: x → v → a by differentiation, a → v → x by integration. You learned to classify acceleration as constant, a(t), or a(x), and to choose the correct integration method. The chain-rule identity a = v dv/dx was your key tool.

Learning Goals for This Lecture
  1. Decompose curved motion into tangential and normal components The tangential component a_t = dv/dt controls speed change. The normal component a_n = v²/ρ controls direction change. These two are always perpendicular and completely independent.
  2. Solve projectile motion by treating x and y as independent Horizontal acceleration is zero (v_x = constant). Vertical acceleration is −g. Solve each axis separately and combine only at the end.
  3. Apply relative velocity as simple vector subtraction v_B/A = v_B − v_A. This answers: 'What does B look like from A's point of view?' Choosing the right reference frame can make a complex problem trivial.
Caution ⚠

Constant speed does NOT mean zero acceleration. A car turning at constant speed has a_n ≠ 0 — direction is changing, so acceleration exists. This is one of the most common errors in this chapter.

Basic 1
Tangential vs Normal — Direction Identification
A car moves along a curved path. At a certain instant its speed is increasing and it is turning left. Describe the directions of: (1) tangential acceleration a_t, and (2) normal acceleration a_n.
Basic 2
Normal Acceleration in Circular Motion
A particle moves in a circle of radius r = 5 m with constant speed v = 10 m/s. Find the normal acceleration.
🟡 Standard Problems — Core
Standard 1
T–N Decomposition with Time-Varying Speed
A particle moves along a curved path with speed v = 4t and radius of curvature ρ = 8. Find the total acceleration at t = 2. Hint: find a_t and a_n separately, then combine.
Standard 2
Projectile at the Highest Point
A projectile is launched at an angle. At the highest point of its trajectory: (1) What is the velocity? (2) What is the acceleration? Treat x and y as independent.
Standard 3
Relative Velocity — Vector Subtraction
Car A moves east at 20 m/s. Car B moves north at 15 m/s. Find the velocity of B relative to A (v_B/A). Hint: v_B/A = v_B − v_A.
🔴 Advanced Problem — Optional
Advanced 1
Relative Motion in a Rotating Frame
Car A moves in a circle of radius R at constant speed v. A passenger inside throws a ball straight upward. Describe the motion of the ball (1) in the inertial frame and (2) relative to the car. What does this tell you about fictitious forces?
